## Who I am

* **Name:** Darman.ai

* **What I do, in one sentence:** We help physicians understand how to use generative AI practically and responsibly in their real clinical work, through short, specialty-specific education led by doctors who are already using these tools.

* **Who I am writing for:**
  Primarily:

  * Hospital and clinic decision-makers responsible for AI adoption, innovation, digital transformation, medical education, and clinical operations
  * CMOs, CMIOs, CIOs, Heads of Innovation, Medical Education leaders, clinical department heads, and hospital executives
  * Private healthcare groups and medical institutions trying to introduce AI across clinical teams without creating unnecessary risk

  Secondarily:

  * Practicing physicians who want to use AI in their specialty, not just experiment with generic prompts
  * Residents and trainees developing their workflows and AI habits early
  * Clinicians who are curious about AI but skeptical of hype

* **What I sell:**
  Darman.ai is an AI education platform for healthcare professionals.

  We create short, practical courses with physicians showing how they actually use generative AI in their specialty and everyday work — including where the tools help, where they fail, and where human judgment still matters.

  For organizations, hospitals and clinics can provide structured AI education to their medical teams.

  For individual clinicians, Darman.ai provides access to specialty-specific courses and practical AI workflows.

  Darman Labs complements the education by giving clinicians a place to actually use selected AI tools and workflows they learn about.

## My positions

Three to five things I believe that some of my audience does not. These are where the good posts come from.

1. **Generic AI education is not enough for clinicians.**
   Knowing how ChatGPT works is different from knowing where AI belongs in a cardiologist's, radiologist's, dermatologist's or surgeon's workflow. Healthcare AI education should be specialty-specific and use-case-driven.

2. **The biggest barrier to healthcare AI adoption isn't access to AI tools. It's knowing when and how to use them.**
   Most clinicians can already open ChatGPT or another AI product. The difficult part is understanding which workflows are appropriate, what information can be trusted, what needs verification, and where AI should not be used.

3. **The limitations of an AI workflow are as important as the workflow itself.**
   Good AI education shouldn't only demonstrate successful examples. Physicians need to understand failure modes, uncertainty, privacy considerations, hallucinations and situations where clinical judgment must take over.

4. **Doctors learn AI better from doctors than from generic AI experts.**
   A technically impressive demonstration isn't necessarily clinically useful. Seeing how another physician applies AI inside a familiar workflow makes the lesson more relevant and easier to evaluate critically.

5. **Hospitals shouldn't treat AI education as a one-time training session.**
   Models, tools and capabilities are changing too quickly. AI literacy needs to become an ongoing part of professional development, with education evolving alongside the technology and its real-world use.

## Off limits

* **Topics I do not post about:**

  * Individual patient diagnoses or treatment recommendations
  * Personal medical advice
  * Patient cases containing identifiable information
  * Political debates unrelated to healthcare AI
  * AI fearmongering or claims that physicians will be replaced
  * Generic AI news with no practical relevance to healthcare professionals
  * Sensational predictions about AGI
  * Competitor attacks
  * Posts designed purely to manufacture controversy
  * AI-generated medical claims presented as established evidence

* **Clients or numbers I cannot name publicly:**
  Do not name hospitals, clinics, physicians, institutional partners, subscriber numbers, revenue, conversion rates, course completion data or other commercial metrics unless they have been explicitly approved for public use.

* **Claims I am not allowed to make:**
  Do not claim that Darman.ai:

  * Improves patient outcomes unless supported by appropriate evidence
  * Reduces medical errors unless demonstrated
  * Makes AI use "safe" in an absolute sense
  * Guarantees regulatory compliance
  * Is HIPAA-, GDPR- or otherwise compliant unless this has been formally verified
  * Saves clinicians a specific number of hours unless measured
  * Increases physician productivity by a specific percentage unless measured
  * Provides clinically validated AI tools unless they have actually been validated
  * Provides CME/CPD credits unless accreditation has been obtained
  * Works with "the world's best doctors" unless there is a defensible methodology behind that statement

  Prefer language such as **responsible AI use**, **safer adoption**, **understanding limitations**, and **supporting clinicians' decision-making** rather than absolute safety or effectiveness claims.

## Proof I can use

Until Darman.ai has enough validated customer and usage data to publish meaningful metrics, use **product and process proof rather than invented performance proof**.

* Our courses are built around real clinical workflows rather than generic AI theory.
* Course content is created with physicians who demonstrate how they use generative AI in their own field.
* The content focuses not only on what works, but also on limitations, verification and situations where AI should not be used.
* Lessons are designed to be short and practical because physicians have limited time for additional training.
* The platform is being designed for both organizational adoption — hospitals, clinics and medical institutions educating their teams — and individual clinicians.
* Darman Labs is designed to connect education with practice, allowing clinicians to interact with AI tools and workflows rather than only learning about them theoretically.
* Darman.ai follows developments in healthcare AI and translates them into information that is relevant to clinicians rather than simply reposting general AI news.
* Whenever we have permission, physician stories and specific workflow examples should become the strongest form of proof: **what the physician was trying to accomplish → how they used AI → what worked → what required verification → what they would or wouldn't use again.**

**Important rule for future content:**
Never invent a number for a stronger hook.

If we don't yet have a statistic, customer result or study to support a statement, make the post strong through specificity, experience and a clear point of view instead.
