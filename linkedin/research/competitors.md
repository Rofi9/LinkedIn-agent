## Patterns

- **"By clinicians, for clinicians"** is everyone's claim. It no longer sets
  Darman apart on its own; specialty and hands-on practice do.
- **"AI literacy, not coding"** is the shared beginner frame.
- **Ethics, bias, regulation** appear in almost every curriculum.
- **Free badge courses** (Google/DiMe, GE, AMA) push paid players toward
  certification and leadership programs.
- **Announcements are about scale:** hours of content, people trained, deals.
- **Tone is institutional and cautious**, with little show-your-screen,
  hands-on content. That's Darman's opening.

## Gaps nobody covers well

1. Specialty-specific generative-AI workflows (outside radiology).
2. Hands-on sandboxes with realistic cases (DiMe's lab is ~1 hour).
3. Regional context: Armenian and Arabic, local rules, CIS and Caucasus.
4. Showing hospital buyers results, not headcounts.
5. Neutral tool comparisons not tied to a vendor.
6. Short content that keeps pace with model releases.
