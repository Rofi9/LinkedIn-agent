# Governments and regulators: AI in healthcare, 2025–2026

Researched 2026-09-28. Most official sites blocked page fetches, so this rests
on search results pointing to official pages. **Open the primary source before
publishing anything marked (verify).** The recurring rule: say whether
something is **binding** or **guidance**, and never imply EU or US rules bind
hospitals in Armenia or the Gulf.

| Where | What's happening | Binding? | Why it matters for training doctors |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EU** | AI Act Art. 4 (AI literacy) applies since 2 Feb 2025; enforceable since 3 Aug 2026. The **Digital Omnibus** (Reg. 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026) softened it from "ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level" to "take measures to support the development of AI literacy", with no required level per person. High-risk medical-device AI deadline pushed to **2 Aug 2028**. | Yes, in the EU | EU hospitals using AI must still make a genuine training effort. Not certificates, not a guaranteed level. |
| **UK** | 10 Year Health Plan (Jul 2025): "most AI-enabled health system". NHS guidance on AI scribes (Apr 2025) expects organizations to say what training clinicians get. National Commission on AI regulation reported 10 Sep 2026 (risk-based rules, staged "L-plate" approvals). BMA called the 2026 workforce plan a "dangerous gamble on AI". | Guidance; device rules binding | Trusts are expected to train staff before rolling out scribes. |
| **US** | FDA loosened oversight of some clinical decision support (CDS) software, Jan 2026 (verify current version). HHS AI Strategy (Dec 2025) has workforce development as a pillar. ONC proposed removing "AI model card" transparency (verify status). Texas laws (Sept 2025 / Jan 2026) require telling patients when AI is used and reviewing AI-generated records. | FDA guidance non-binding; Texas laws binding in Texas | Lighter federal oversight shifts judgment onto the clinician. |
| **WHO** | Guidance on large multimodal models (2024). WHO/Europe (Nov 2025): only **4 of 50** countries surveyed have a health-specific AI strategy; urges workforce training on AI basics, ethics, data. EU edition Apr 2026. | Guidance | A neutral reference for any country without national rules, including Armenia. |
| **UAE** | Abu Dhabi DoH Responsible AI Standard, effective Oct 2025 (transparency, explainability, patient opt-out, security). Global AI Healthcare Academy. Dubai: AI policy since 2021; staff AI training since Apr 2025. | DoH standards generally mandatory for Abu Dhabi facilities (verify) | Abu Dhabi hospitals now have a written standard staff must understand. **No official source says AI training is mandatory for UAE doctors. Don't claim it.** |
| **Saudi Arabia** | SFDA guidance on AI/ML medical devices (2023) and MDS-G027 (Aug 2025, verify title). SDAIA × King Saud University trained 100+ practitioners (Jan 2025). No SCFHS requirement for AI CPD found. | Device registration binding; training voluntary | Regulator active on devices; doctor training still voluntary and project-based. |
| **Qatar** | MoPH × HBKU research guidelines for healthcare AI; national Ethical AI principles (2024; effective May 2025, verify); National Health Strategy 2024–2030. | Guidelines | Ethical-use framework without training requirements. |
| **Armenia** | No health AI strategy or doctor AI-training requirement found. National AI push (AI factory / data-center plan, 2025; AI in education). Experts say a formal strategy is missing (verify 2026). E-health system ArMed; e-prescribing from 2025; universal health insurance from 1 Jan 2026. Health Minister presented the first AI lab-data tool and said medical-data law needs changes. | Nothing AI-specific | A policy gap, so voluntary, WHO-aligned training is the likely route. |
| **South Korea** | AI Basic Act in force 22 Jan 2026; healthcare is a "high-impact" sector with impact-assessment and safety duties; fines grace period to ~Jan 2027. | Binding | An example of a country treating health AI as high-impact by law. |

## Note for the DD-26 series

`evidence.md` quotes the **original** Article 4 wording ("to their best
extent, a sufficient level"). The Digital Omnibus replaced it on 27 Jul 2026.
None of the five posts quote Article 4, but anything new that does must use
the amended wording.
